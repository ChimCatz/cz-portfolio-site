World data volume · 1968–2030

The world's data has grown 47,000-fold since 1986

That works out to a doubling about every 2.4 years, from 2.6 exabytes in 1986 to 123 zettabytes in 2023. Each wave of technology pushed it higher: the web, social media and smartphones, mobile internet, and now AI.

    Dashed: estimate (before 1986) or forecast (after 2023) Log scale: each gridline is 10× the one below

    Reading the eras

    Each coloured band is an era of technology, labeled with how much the world's data multiplied during it. The 2004–2009 band is the steepest: social media, online video, the cloud and the iPhone multiplied data about 14× in six years.

    Part of that jump comes from switching sources (see right). Creation counts every copy and stream, so it runs higher than storage. The rise is real, but the size of this step is overstated.

    How the line is built

    • 1986–2007: the world's capacity to store information (Hilbert & López), with in-between years filled in along the same curve.
    • 2010–2028: data created, captured, copied and consumed each year (Statista, based on IDC). 2024–2028 are forecasts.
    • 1968–1985 and 2029–30: extended from the ends of the data at 20% and 26% a year.
